Objectives

How does cycling network expansion impacts…

  • Trip efficiency: duration, distance, circuity ⏱️
  • Safety: Level of Traffic Stress exposure 🛡️
  • Accessibility: 15-min potential to reach opportunities 🏫
  • Continuity: number of interruptions when using CI 🛑

Goals:

  • Analyze 10 years of evolution (2016–2026) across 50 cities
  • Estimate model elasticities of infrastructure improvements
  • Distinguish effects by infrastructure type (protected, painted, sharrows, shared foot)

OD Pairs Sampling

  • 10km buffer from city center coordinates (20km diameter)

  • 20,000 OD pairs generated per city

  • Destinations randomly sampled, weighted by building volume (proxy for trip attraction)

  • Distance follows a log-normal function calibrated for cycling trips (Kou and Cai 2019)

  • Origin hexagons (with buildings) sampled using H3 resolution 9 (~200m edge length, ~400m “diameter”, ~0.105 km²)

Trip distance distribution

  • Log-normal calibration ensures routes resemble realistic urban cycling trips.

About 75% of trips are below 5 km. This already accounts for ~40% circuity.

CI Classification in 4 types

With osmactive custom classification of CI, we classified OSM segments into 4 simplified types to assess infrastructure quality, based on OSM tags:

Type OSM examples
Protected (separated) highway:cycleway → separated tracks
Painted (medium) cycleway:lane → bike lanes on road
Sharrows (weak/mixed) cycleway:shared_lane → advisory
Foot (shared w/pedestrian) highway:pedestrian & bicycle:yes → shared paths 


-- Conceptual query (based on osmactive logic)
CASE WHEN highway = 'cycleway' OR cycleway IN ('track', 'opposite_track') THEN 'Protected'
     WHEN cycleway IN ('lane', 'opposite_lane') THEN 'Painted'
     WHEN cycleway IN ('share_busway', 'shared_lane') OR bicycle_road = 'yes' THEN 'Sharrows'
     WHEN highway IN ('path', 'footway', 'pedestrian') AND bicycle IN ('yes', 'designated')
          THEN 'Foot'
END AS ci_type

Level of Traffic Stress (LTS)

  • Classification from Mineta Transport Institute (2012)

LTS - Alta Planning

  • Adapted by Conveyal (Conway 2015) to applied to every OSM edge as:
If way does not allow cars → LTS 1
If highway:residential / living_street → LTS 1
If maxspeed < 40 km/h AND lanes < 4 → LTS 2
If highway:tertiary/unclassified + cycleway tag → LTS 2
If cycleway tag on busier road → LTS 3
Otherwise → LTS 4

r5r routing package

r5r R package (Pereira et al. 2021) for bicycle routing, relies on LTS of the highways (and not exactly CI):

library(r5r)
trips <- detailed_itineraries(
    r5r_network = r5_engine,   # city × year street network
    origins = origins_hex,     # same od pairs used across all years
    destinations = dests_hex,
    mode = "BICYCLE",
    max_lts = lts_level,       # 1, 2, 3, or 4
    osm_link_ids = TRUE        # match segments with LTS
)
  • max_lts limits route choice to low-stress paths.
  • If no connected low-stress route exists: R5 penalises the segment by setting speed to walking pace (3.6 km/h) instead of cycling (12 km/h) → 3× time penalty (cost).

CI Interruptions

Quantify network fragmentation at the route level (physical gaps)

  • For each route, identify transitions from CI \(\rightarrow\) non-CI \(\rightarrow\) CI.
  • When consecutive non-CI edges are > 100m, that counts as 1 interruption.

Gaps < 100m are considered negligible transitions (e.g. crossing a safe junction).

Accessibility – 15-min potential

  • For each origin (H3 hex), we compute the sum of building volumes reachable within a 15-minute cycling trip.
  • Increases when more destinations become reachable, or existing destinations become fast enough to reach with low stress.

A new CI makes trips virtually faster (or possible), increasing the accessibility of destinations.

Other concepts

Circuity

\[\text{Circuity} = \frac{\text{Route Length}}{\text{Euclidean Distance (O-D)}}\]

A circuity of 1.4 means that the route is 40% longer than the distance as the crown flies.


Stress score (route)

\[\text{Route Avg LTS} = \frac{1(\%\text{LTS}_1) + 2(\%\text{LTS}_2) + 3(\%\text{LTS}_3) + 4(\%\text{LTS}_4)}{100}\]

The stress score is a weighted LTS [1-4] and is higher for higher “use” of higher LTS segments, in that route.

Modelling

What are the systemic and route-level impacts of adding 1 km of cycling infrastructure on trip performance, safety, and accessibility?


Fixed Effects Panel Regression across Macro (city-wide) and Micro (route-level) scales.

This is a longitudinal spatial panel, not a cross-sectional snapshot. We are watching cities grow over a decade.

Macro Model (City-Wide)

Systemic impact of network expansion on city averages.

\[\log(Y_{ct}) = \beta_1 \cdot \log(\text{Total CI}_{ct}) + \beta_2 \cdot \log(\text{Total Road}_{ct}) + \alpha_c + \tau_t + \varepsilon_{ct}\]


  • \(Y_{ct}\): city-wide average outcome (avg trip duration, avg accessibility) in city \(c\), year \(t\)
  • \(\alpha_c\): city fixed effects — absorbs unobserved time-invariant city traits (geography, road grid, morphology)
  • \(\tau_t\): year fixed effects — absorbs global shocks (COVID infrastructure boom, OSM tagging growth)
  • Standard Errors clustered by city; N = 250 obs (50 cities × 5 years)

The log-log specification gives us elasticities directly: a 1% increase in X → β% change in Y. The FE approach means we are not comparing Amsterdam to São Paulo; we watch each city change relative to itself.

Building CI doesn’t only make trips faster — it makes them possible.

CI and road network expansion explains ~50% of the within-city variation in accessibility over the decade (after controlling for FE).

Micro Model (Route-Level)

Impacts of infrastructure on individual route characteristics.

\[Y_{rt} = \beta_1 \text{Protected}_{rt} + \beta_2 \text{Paint}_{rt} + \beta_3 \text{Sharrow}_{rt} + \beta_4 \text{Foot}_{rt} + \gamma_{r} + \tau_t + \varepsilon_{rt}\]

  • \(Y_{rt}\): transformation depends on the outcome:
    • log for duration & circuity → semi-elasticity
    • level for % LTS1, avg. stress score, interruptions → marginal effect
  • \(\text{Prot}_{rt}\), etc.: km of CI type along route \(r\) in year \(t\) (always in levels)
  • \(\gamma_{r}\): route fixed effect — compares the same OD pairs’ routes accross the years
  • \(\tau_t\): time fixed effect — absorbs global OSM tagging trends
  • SE clustered by city; N ≈ 4.95M route-year observations

The route fixed effect is the key advantage. We are not comparing a flat downtown trip to a hilly suburb. We are watching the exact same OD pair change as the city builds infrastructure. This controls for all time-invariant route geometry.

The cyclist trade-off 🔄

Per 1 km of protected CI built:

  • Circuity +~6%
    (rider detours to use the safe path)

  • Effective trip duration −~1.3%
    (safer route avoids slow mixed-traffic friction)

Cyclists willingly accept a longer detour for a “faster”, safer trip.

The Arterial Lure 🛣️

Stress penalty (Avg. LTS increase) by weaker infrastructure type

  • Per 1km Protected, the % of route using safe streets (LTS1) drops 2pp*
  • But for 1km Painted lane, it is 7x worse!
  • Paint attracts to higher stress streets
  • Sharrows are usually placed in (already) quiet streets
  • * the cyclist still has to use other segments to access the protected CI

Separation is real. Protected CI are less stressfull than non-protected

Before the painted lane existed, the routing engine avoided that arterial road entirely. It kept the rider on quiet backstreets (LTS 1). Once the cycleway=lane tag is added, the engine says, “Oh, this is ‘safe’ enough to use now, let’s take the shortcut!” It lures the route onto the arterial.

OSM drawing and tagging

Protected CI is drawn as a separate segment in OSM. Painted lanes and sharrows are tags on the road segment. (Both approaches are modelled correctly.)

  • Paint → rider shares the arterial’s space → inherits its danger
  • Protection → spatially separate → implies a road diet or space-reallocation (lanes, parking, etc.)

Findings

Key Takeaways

Finding Metric Effect
Network growth eliminates impedance Avg. duration (macro) −1.45% per 10% CI growth
Network unlocks destinations 15-min accessibility +3.4% per 10% CI growth
Detour as trade-off for safety Circuity (micro) +6% per 1km protected CI
Trip is (virtually?) faster Duration (micro) −1.3% per 1km prot. CI
Only protected CI closes gaps Interruptions −0.084 per km (signif.)

Infrastructure works. But isolated corridors without gap-closing create a fragmented, stressful network.
The win lies in continuity and protection, not just quantity.

Limitations

Routing Engine

  • Calm residential roads (LTS 1 by default) are treated the same as dedicated CI → engine may route through dense neighbourhoods rather than CI.
  • Custom cost schemes in R5 are simple and objective; standard LTS was used to maintain reproducibility and comparability.

OSM Data Quality

  • Tagging evolution: OSM segments are tagged differently across years — our algorithm captures some of this, but tagging changes ≠ physical infrastructure changes (e.g., Munich 2021→2026). Also, cycling tags change.
  • Contributions growth: OSM data density in all cities increased 2016→2026
  • Delays in OSM: some “new CI” may just be newly mapped old CI.
  • Segment fragmentation: Protected CI drawn as separate segments is realistic, but increases with tagging evolution.

These routing-engine and OSM limitations would affect any study using these data sources — they are inherent to using open, longitudinal data globally.

The “Fragmentation Phase” Paradox ⚠️

At the macro level, total CI positively predicts interruptions (β ≈ +0.656, ***):

Expanding the total network by 1% adds a statistically significant, but practically small, 0.006 interruptions to the average commute. While small per trip, the positive direction confirms that cities historically expand via disconnected, opportunistic segments.

Why? Cities in 2016–2026 were in the “Fragmented Network Phase”:

  1. No CI (Stage 1): Rider uses mixed traffic → 0 interruptions
  2. Fragmented CI (Stage 2): Isolated islands; each new segment creates 2 new gap edges
  3. Mature network (Stage 3): Connected corridors → interruptions drop

Policy implication: Shift success metrics from “km built” → “gaps closed”
